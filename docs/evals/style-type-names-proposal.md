# Proposed names for silhouette and visual impression

Draft for Qing’s review, 1 November 2026. These are **our proposed wardrobe stories**, not established human types, a validated instrument, a production taxonomy or an assignment to any person. Keep the appeal of the 31 palette names without inventing a complete grid of people.

The proposal has two independently usable menus: six silhouette grammars and four visual-impression overlays. They are starting points for coherent outfits. A person may explore several, combine regional constructions, reject all, or keep a useful description without a name. Colour, taste and comfort remain separate.

## Evidence and interpretation

The earlier factor research is recorded at repository commit `10da7f9`, `docs/evals/style-factor-research.md`; its working path was subsequently reused. It distinguishes source claims from proposed decompositions and reports the limits of its visual probe. No new visual experiment or paid call supports this naming draft.

For the historical crosswalk, [Body Shape Test](https://bodyshapetest.org/kibbe/) describes its own adaptation of thirteen historical labels; its questions, reference patterns and scoring are explicitly unvalidated editorial choices. That is evidence of this adaptation, not a current official Kibbe assessment. [Kibbe’s publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/723284/david-kibbes-power-of-style-by-david-kibbe/) describes an updated system in the 2025 book. We are not reproducing that identification method.

For essence language, [The Concept Wardrobe](https://theconceptwardrobe.com/7-style-essences/complete-style-essences-guides) supplies clothing descriptions in its interpretation of seven essences. They are attributed style claims. [Kitchener’s own account](https://www.pscjohnkitchener.com/meet-john-kitchener) allows multiple harmonies; this proposal imposes no three-essence cap. No source here establishes scientific natural kinds or percentages of a person.

## Factor map to preserve underneath the names

| Information | Keep separately | What it must not silently become |
|---|---|---|
| Regional geometry | Length, breadth, edge definition, slope, scale/fineness and contour/fullness; record region, image/view and uncertainty | One global yin–yang value; hidden measurements; a face standing in for the torso |
| Length evidence | Reported height/measurements, perceived elongation and clothing-created continuity | Interchangeable measures of “vertical” |
| Garment response | Support, seam placement, sleevehead, openings/lapels, ease, waist transitions, hem/coat length, rise/seat/leg/break | A body fact inferred from one successful jacket |
| Material and composition | Weight, stiffness, drape, surface, detail scale/density/location, symmetry, rhythm and continuity | Verified fibre content from an image; every factor required in every consultation |
| Visual impression / essence | Tentative holistic descriptions: precise, relaxed, flowing, ornate, playful; preserve unmodelled language | Personality, maturity, gender, innocence, temperament or compulsory colour choices |
| Personal criteria | Taste, desired expression, comfort, occasion, constraints and optional wardrobe | Evidence of anatomy or proof of visual harmony |

Regions remain independent: shoulders/neck, torso, waist/abdomen/hips, arms/legs, extremities and facial features. Upper-body support and a fluid lower line can coexist. Unknown stays unknown. Moderation requires affirmative evidence; opposing local features do not cancel into “balanced.”

## Source-described regions: a crosswalk, not a conversion rule

The label column identifies the adaptation’s vocabulary. The factor reading is **our interpretation** of its descriptions, retaining locations and uncertainty rather than adopting its score. No numerical thresholds are proposed.

| Historical label in that adaptation | Our factor reading |
|---|---|
| Dramatic | Elongation; sharp edges |
| Soft Dramatic | Elongation/edge versus fuller contours |
| Flamboyant Natural | Breadth; blunt edges; elongation |
| Natural | Breadth versus blunt edges |
| Soft Natural | Blunt structure versus rounded contours |
| Dramatic Classic | Moderation versus local angularity |
| Classic | Affirmed moderation by region |
| Soft Classic | Moderation versus soft contours |
| Flamboyant Gamine | Compactness; located angular contrasts |
| Gamine | Small scale versus angularity |
| Soft Gamine | Compact scale; contrast; roundness |
| Theatrical Romantic | Fullness/fineness versus local sharpness |
| Romantic | Rounded fullness versus fine structure |

These source descriptions do not establish a compulsory wardrobe or our new names. In particular, breadth is not bluntness and fineness is not lack of fullness.

For the seven essence descriptions, the clothing-region translation below is also our decomposition. Their familiar labels identify the source language, not names we propose to ship.

| Source essence | Clothing tendencies described by the guide | Factors to test independently |
|---|---|---|
| Dramatic | Extended, directional, structured | Length; edge; stiffness; scale |
| Natural | Relaxed, layered, irregular | Ease; layering; surface; shape |
| Classic | Moderate, restrained, gently fitted | Scale; symmetry; ease; density |
| Gamine | Compact, interrupted, angular | Segment length; edge; rhythm |
| Ingenue | Small rounded decorative details | Scale; curvature; ornament density |
| Romantic | Rounded, wrapping, tactile | Contour; drape; surface; detail |
| Angelic | Extended, fine, flowing | Length; flow; fineness; transparency |

We do not import the guide’s associated personality, gender, age or palette claims. A precise composition can use pale colours; playfulness need not use high chroma. A person can like ornate details while preferring restrained outfits.

## Missing combinations to keep available

These are counterexamples proposed by the factor research and this draft, not newly discovered population types:

- Broad **and** sharp; narrow **and** blunt; broad **and** sloping shoulders.
- Long limbs with fine extremities; different arm/leg relationships; broad shoulders with small hands.
- Fine structure with full contours; angular jaw with rounded cheeks; small-scale full features.
- Compact rounded shapes with sparse ornament; compact angular shapes with restraint.
- Extended curves in substantial opaque cloth; broad, flowing construction with a polished finish.
- Different needs above and below the waist; a local accent repeating a facial feature without dictating the whole outfit.

No new name is required for every combination. Preserve the description and choose a useful comparison.

## Ten proposed names

Names describe outfit grammars and effects, not a person’s anatomy. Every row below is **our design proposal and an untested clothing hypothesis**. The comparison should preserve the wearer, pose, lighting and unrelated garment variables where possible.

### Silhouette menu

| Name | Intended grammar / factor region | Appealing clothing hypothesis to compare |
|---|---|---|
| **Spire** | Extended directional line; edge definition independent of breadth | A longer clean jacket–trouser line versus a shorter jacket, keeping width and detail stable. Direction does not require heavy cloth. |
| **Arcade** | Extended line with deliberate curves and transitions | A curved seam or wrapping transition versus a straighter one in a similarly long composition. Flow may use opaque, substantial cloth. |
| **Pavilion** | Supported upper construction with usable ease; lower regions remain open | A supported versus softer shoulder at comparable fit, retaining a comfortable lower silhouette. Fine cuffs or jewellery can coexist with substantial upper construction. |
| **Atrium** | Affirmed moderate scale and controlled transitions | A measured lapel/opening or garment length versus one changed scale. This is not the default when evidence is missing or contradictory. |
| **Mosaic** | Compact segments with intentional contrast or interruption | A shorter layer and visible break versus continuity, retaining comparable volume. Contrast can be about shape rather than colour. |
| **Cameo** | Compact rounded line; ornament optional | Rounded versus more directional collar or edge shapes at the same scale. Sparse detail remains a full option. |

### Visual-impression menu

| Name | Intended effect / factor region | Optional expression to compare |
|---|---|---|
| **Chisel** | Precise, sparse, directional | Repeat a crisp edge in opening and accessory versus a gentler edge, preserving silhouette. Precision can be soft, light or colourful. |
| **Lilt** | Rhythmic, lightly playful | A small repeated accent or asymmetric punctuation versus a quiet version. No claim about youth, personality or required bright colours. |
| **Filigree** | Fine curvilinear ornament | A bounded area of curved detail versus a plain surface, holding cut and cloth behaviour close. Detail need not cover the entire look. |
| **Haze** | Diffuse, flowing, fine surface | A softer transition or finer surface versus a clearer one, retaining coverage and comparable fit. Transparency and pale colour are optional. |

This is **not a 6×4 taxonomy**. “Pavilion with Filigree detail” is one intelligible outfit proposal, not a new person category. Upper Pavilion / lower Arcade can remain an explicit regional description. The menus deliberately leave space for an unnamed relaxed, textured or substantial impression; these ten names need not exhaust what the stylist can say.

## How to put this in front of Qing

Show two or three coherent named outfit stories on the same person, with one sentence on the interaction being explored. Ask separately: *Does the visual effect work? Do you like it? Would you wear it comfortably? Is the rendering faithful?* Both, neither and uncertain remain useful answers. A rejected image can reject the rendering, a garment, or a proposed mechanism without defining the person.

Review the names for appeal and useful distinction before deciding how many to keep. No personal type has been assigned, no source label is a hidden answer, and none of these proposals is production-frozen.
