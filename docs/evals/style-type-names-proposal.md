# Opal Line & Presence: complete proposed style atlas

Review draft, 1 November 2026. **21 Line profiles and 14 Presence profiles: all 13 historical silhouette references and all seven essence references have distinct names, plus eight silhouette and seven presence extensions.** This replaces the rejected six-silhouette/four-overlay proposal. Names describe the visible idea in ordinary words; they are not restricted to single words.

The colour precedent is expansion with preservation: 31 colour templates include the conventional twelve. Here every source anchor also survives individually. Unlike colour's three-coordinate construction, style has regional factors and overlapping profiles; these 35 style entries are not a disjoint grid, population census, or 21×14 set of compulsory combinations. They are proposed named types for exploration, with a signature rich enough to explain the name. No empirical validation or automatic assignment is claimed.

**Line** describes silhouette and the regional construction it invites us to test. **Presence** describes visual character, independently of silhouette. A final personal style can use several local observations to become one coherent named aesthetic. A source label is a reference, not a hidden correct answer.

## Source scope and attribution

[Kibbe's own site](https://davidkibbe.co/about/) describes a ten-archetype Image Identity system. The historical thirteen-label vocabulary retained below additionally includes Natural, Classic and Gamine as individual anchors. Those three are explicitly marked historical; retaining them is our coverage decision, not a claim that they remain current official identities. The [2025 publisher description](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/723284/david-kibbes-power-of-style-by-david-kibbe/) describes an updated system. We do not reproduce its identification method.

The short source cues below come from the historical vocabulary as represented by [Body Shape Test's independent adaptation](https://bodyshapetest.org/kibbe/), whose scoring is editorial and unvalidated. They are deliberately separate from **our proposed factor signatures and garment experiments**. These are not authoritative definitions of Kibbe identities. Historical coverage means preserving every label and its meaningful contrast, not claiming a lossless translation of the entire original theory.

For the seven essence anchors, [The Concept Wardrobe](https://theconceptwardrobe.com/7-style-essences/complete-style-essences-guides) supplies its own clothing interpretation. [Kitchener's own account](https://www.pscjohnkitchener.com/meet-john-kitchener) names Angelic/ethereal and permits four or more, even all seven harmonies. We retain Angelic and Ethereal as aliases of one source anchor, not two types. We import neither a three-essence limit nor personality, age, gender or palette claims.

## Factors beneath the names

Keep **length, breadth, edge definition, slope, local scale/fineness, and contour/fullness** separate by region: neck/shoulder, torso, waist/abdomen/hips, arms, legs, hands/feet and face. Record image/view, uncertainty and provenance; unknown is not moderate. Reported height, apparent elongation and garment-created continuity are different observations. A long arm does not establish long legs; broad shoulders do not establish broad hands. Balanced requires affirmative moderation in relevant regions, not cancellation of extremes.

Separately record garment support, seam/sleevehead, ease, opening/lapel, waist transition, length/rise/break; cloth weight, stiffness, flow and surface; and composition scale, density, symmetry and rhythm. Presence is a tentative reading of visual character, not a temperament score. Taste, desired expression, comfort, occasion and wardrobe needs each retain their own evidence. The body does not acquire a new factor because a garment was liked.

The three Compact profiles preserve the source's distinctions: angular-dominant contrast, mixed sharp/round contrast, and round-dominant contrast. The three Balanced profiles preserve moderate-with-angular, moderate, and moderate-with-curved. They must not collapse back into one Compact or Balanced result.

## Line: eight explicit gap extensions

These have **no one-to-one source equivalent**. Each makes a previously coupled factor combination directly nameable. They can overlap an anchor; their value is a distinctive question and construction response, not manufactured exclusivity. Named extensions do not exclude unlisted combinations.

| ID | New Line name | Proposed signature / gap preserved | Nearest anchor and decisive distinction | Useful clothing experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L14 | **Broad & Sharply Defined** | Broad shoulders/upper frame AND sharp local corners | Long & Broad / Broad & Easy associate breadth with bluntness; this retains sharpness independently and imposes no elongation | Preserve shoulder room while comparing defined vs rounded sleevehead/opening |
| L15 | **Narrow & Blunt-Edged** | Narrow upper frame AND blunt, substantial-looking local edges | Long & Sculpted suggests sharpness; Broad & Easy suggests breadth; neither represents this conjunction directly | A narrow straight outer line with rounded substantial collar vs pointed collar |
| L16 | **Broad & Sloping** | Upper breadth AND descending shoulder slope; edge sharpness separate | Broad & Easy leaves slope unstated; this makes support and neckline transition the central question | Same body ease with natural slope vs supported shoulder; assess neck/shoulder transition |
| L17 | **Long & Fine-Framed** | Long limbs/continuity AND fine wrists/ankles/local scale; no required sharpness | Long & Sculpted foregrounds edge; Long & Broad foregrounds breadth; here length and delicate extremities coexist | Long line with fine cuff/hem finish vs substantial finish; preserve torso ease |
| L18 | **Structured Above, Flowing Below** | Upper region benefits from support while lower region benefits from fluid continuity | Long & Curved does not specify opposed regional construction; this explicitly bridges them without averaging | Supported jacket plus flowing trousers/skirt vs uniform stiffness; keep upper cut stable |
| L19 | **Compact & Smoothly Rounded** | Compact scale with rounded transitions and no required sharp/round interruption or lush fullness | Compact & Rounded Contrast retains interruption; Curved & Rounded centres fuller contours; this requires neither | Short rounded clean layer with smooth continuity vs crisp segment break; sparse detail held fixed |
| L20 | **Flowing Above, Structured Below** | Upper line benefits from soft ease while lower line benefits from controlled shape/support | Reverse of L18; neither regional response is inferred from the other | Fluid shirt or knit over a defined waistband and crisp trouser line vs fluidity throughout |
| L21 | **Broad Shoulders & Fine Extremities** | Broad upper frame with fine wrists/hands and/or ankles/feet; each observed separately | Broad & Easy does not preserve extremity scale; Long & Fine-Framed requires length that this does not | Shoulder room with fine cuffs/finishes vs uniformly substantial finishing; do not shrink the whole garment |

L14–L17 and L21 are regional geometry-led extensions; L18 and L20 are regional response-led extensions; L19 is a scale/contour composition extension. The catalogue says which kind of evidence each requires. Do not infer anatomy from L18's successful outfit, or pretend all entries are equivalent body measurements.

All seven specify a combination missing from the seven anchor descriptions, not a claim that the source systems forbid such clothes. P12 is a deliberate design addition; the other extensions directly expose coupled factors in the research map. These are hypotheses to test. Multiple presences can coexist with no forced percentage sum or maximum of three.

## Menswear and other domains

Select garment domain explicitly, never from gender or a face. The historical silhouette anchors are cross-references, not a men's body quiz imported from women's descriptions. In adult menswear translate observations into shoulder support/slope, sleevehead/armhole, collar opening, lapel/gorge, chest ease, button stance and coat length, then trouser rise/seat/thigh/leg/break. Broad & Curved might invite chest ease and a gradual jacket waist transition; Curved & Finely Angular might invite a softly shaped line and fine crisp collar. These are experiments, not equivalences between anatomy and a suit pattern.

All 21 Line and 14 Presence proposals are available for adult menswear exploration. Lush & Sensuous can mean soft lustrous shirting, tactile knitwear or a curved scarf arrangement; Delicate & Decorative can mean fine embroidery, small rounded hardware or a patterned neckerchief; Airy & Flowing can mean a fine long overshirt. Suits, ties, femininity and specific colours are never prerequisites. Streetwear, workwear, traditional garments and knitwear need their own construction examples, rather than tailoring terms forced onto them.

Other adult garment domains use regional neckline/shoulder/torso/waist/hip/limb lines, support, volume and drape, with equal scope for tailoring and softness. **Child essence is a separate feature:** playful aesthetic stories and comfort, never adult silhouette/body typing or this adult sensual vocabulary. Colour remains independently reusable.

## Worked composition and review checks

A hypothetical adult has observed upper breadth, uncertain shoulder slope, long arms and fine wrists. They like smooth surfaces and dislike ornate detail. Do not average this into Balanced, assume long legs, or make dislike evidence of anatomy. Compare **Broad & Sharply Defined** only if sharp upper edges are actually visible, and **Long & Fine-Framed** only where the regional evidence supports it. A **Polished & Relaxed** expression could use a smooth overshirt with shoulder room and a fine cuff. A second composition could test shoulder support with the same lower line. Decide from the whole outfit; do not concatenate every named profile into a costume.

Ask separately about visual harmony, taste, practical suitability and rendering fidelity; wardrobe complementarity joins when relevant. Preserve both/neither/uncertain and free text. A drawn shoulder error updates rendering instructions, not body geometry. The user decides when the coherent personal aesthetic is satisfactory.

Coverage check: **13/13 historical silhouette labels, including 10/10 current labels; 7/7 essence anchors; eight and seven named extensions respectively.** No source type is dropped or merged in the crosswalk. All new names have distinct factor descriptions and neighbour contrasts. Local modifiers still preserve different arm/leg relationships, angular jaw with rounded cheeks, and small-scale full features without forcing them into a whole-body class. No scientific validation, population completeness, exclusivity, precise cutoffs or successful on-person trial is asserted. Open work is comparative testing and name appeal, not finishing missing source coverage.
