# Visual colour hypotheses: two public portraits

These are native visual observations, not app output, measured colour factors, a season assignment, or an accuracy evaluation. The images are edited public images; no additional photographs or user reports were checked. Screen colours, light, white balance, makeup, hair treatment, compression and processing can all alter the impression. A response to a colour is also not the same thing as a preference for wearing it.

This is **not a blind evaluation**. The first image itself contains an editorial season label and a potentially recognizable person. The general private design guidance read for this probe also included named examples with published season claims, so the observer had label exposure before making these observations. Neither those claims nor the image label is treated as ground truth, and no classification accuracy is claimed.

## Reference 1: public four-panel portrait collage

[View the supplied image](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0729/6214/6631/files/Neutral_Pastel_Aesthetic_Vintage_Moodboard_Photo_Collage_Grid_Instagram_Post_28_480x480.webp?v=1721664854)

The image shows light-looking hair and skin in several different views. The apparent skin and hair colour shifts between panels, and some panels have stronger shadows and shinier pink clothing. This is not a controlled comparison; even whether the hair is its natural colour is unknown.

- **Temperature:** No reliable direction from this image. A tentative warm-versus-cool question remains open: visible golden tones may come from hair treatment, lighting or makeup. Repeated preference for one temperature when saturation and depth are closely matched would challenge the other direction.
- **Value:** Light-to-medium colours are a reasonable *starting hypothesis* for exploration because the visible hair and skin appear relatively light in several panels. It is not established that deeper colours overwhelm the face. If matched deeper colours repeatedly preserve facial definition better, revise this hypothesis.
- **Chroma:** Unknown. Soft and clearer colours could both work; the collage contains pink garments but does not control their depth, lighting or fabric sheen. Repeated same-family comparisons favouring either clean or softened colour would narrow the question.
- **Contrast:** Apparent facial/hair contrast looks low to moderate in some views, less certain in the darker panel. A more evenly lit image with natural hair visible, or comparisons showing greater distinction without overpowering the face, could challenge that impression. This is an image observation, not an independent body measurement.

**Highest-value next comparison:** near the same unchanged face photo, compare a cool rose and a warm coral of as similar lightness and saturation as practical, with matte swatches and unchanged crop. Ask which seems clearer on the face, allowing both, neither or unsure. This probes the unresolved temperature question while reducing, but not eliminating, changes in other colour dimensions. Repeat in another hue family and lighting before treating a direction as robust.

## Reference 2: public head-and-shoulders portrait

[View the supplied image](https://image.tmdb.org/t/p/w185/dgTD7Q9owL3DyX9uYoSX9KZeUUz.jpg)

The rendered image shows dark-looking hair and brows, lighter-looking skin and blue-looking eyes. The white shirt, bright background, directional shadows and small image size make the visible contrast conspicuous; none establish natural, unprocessed colouring.

- **Temperature:** Unknown. The skin appears somewhat warm or reddish in this particular rendering, but that can reflect illumination, exposure or processing rather than undertone. Both warm and cool options remain plausible; consistent matched comparisons favouring the other direction would disconfirm either provisional choice.
- **Value:** Medium-to-deep colours are worth testing as an initial hypothesis because of the dark hair and brows. That does not prove a deep palette is best: repeated preference for matched lighter alternatives without loss of facial definition would challenge it.
- **Chroma:** Unknown. The visible eye/hair separation could support testing clearer colours, but contrast is not the same as saturation tolerance. If softened, same-depth versions consistently support the face better than clear versions, the clear-colour hypothesis weakens.
- **Contrast:** Apparent hair-to-skin contrast is fairly strong in this photo. The white shirt, shadow and possible retouching can exaggerate it. If an evenly lit, unprocessed reference shows less separation, or lower-contrast combinations repeatedly work better, revise the impression.

**Highest-value next comparison:** compare a cool blue-red and a warm tomato-red of closely matched depth and saturation beside the same unchanged face photo. This explores the uncertain temperature axis without using the dark hair as a proxy for undertone; repeat with a second hue family and note that perfect matching is not assured.
