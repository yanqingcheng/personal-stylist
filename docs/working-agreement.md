# How we work for this hackathon

## Objective and product authority

We are here to win the hackathon by delivering the distinctive experience the product owner envisions. Her vision, taste and judgement of what is cool and good enough guide product choices. Agents implement, investigate facts, make proposals visible early and explain concrete constraints. Do not substitute a generic safer-looking product, smaller hidden requirement, or agent aesthetic preference for her intent. Winning is the objective, not a promised outcome.

## The loop for each increment

1. Identify the user intent served, using current-decisions.md and the relevant original notes. Separate explicit decisions from our interpretation. If the intent is already documented, use it rather than asking again.
2. Propose the smallest concrete slice that demonstrates it. State material assumptions early so the owner can redirect while independent work continues. No silent narrowing of acceptance: continued exploration until satisfaction or budget pause stays that way.
3. Assign a bounded bead with owner, file scope, dependencies, stopping boundary and observable outcome. Parallelise UI, infrastructure, prompts and experiments where independent; prevent overlapping writers. Commit and push recoverable checkpoints.
4. Show actual screenshots or generated examples promptly, labelled if synthetic. Ask for judgement on concrete work; do not make the owner log in and traverse the app for every visual decision. When she chooses to test the flow herself, make access straightforward.
5. Review the result against intent, not just code correctness. State which requested experience is demonstrated, what is missing or different, and whether that difference was accepted. A build passing, one generated picture, or one revision is not proof of the consultation experience.
6. Apply her feedback, perform proportionate verification, integrate and continue. Capture new decisions in the authoritative entry point and communicate to affected workers; prevent stale plans from silently regaining authority.

## What we can simplify

Hackathon work may use narrow implementations, manual operations, labelled precomputed demo assets and clearly disabled unfinished features. Skip exhaustive edge-case suites, speculative abstractions, generic frameworks, repeated unchanged checks and internal-state tests with no meaningful user consequence. Use the least testing that provides credible evidence for the actual risk. Do not manufacture false results, expose private material or enable unbounded spend to save time.

## What we cannot cut

Review against the owner's intent is mandatory. The owner judges product appeal and good enough; agents distinguish that judgement from facts about implemented behaviour, privacy and money. Bring material tradeoffs to her promptly with a concrete recommendation, not an invented approval ceremony. Never quietly declare an unmet requirement completed. Keep original evidence, uncertainty, user preferences and model interpretation distinct.

## Coordination and communication

Astra parent stays available for high-level reasoning, requirements and product conversation. Terra leads implementation/integration with bounded workers. Sol reviews material increments and runs focused prompt/evaluation work. Astra design owns the homepage visual direction. Use the existing shared beads tracker; no extra process system. Exact ownership can change when explicitly handed over.

No avoidable downtime: when a task is blocked, continue independent work and tell the owner immediately what is needed, what it blocks and what continues. Do not leave workers waiting on decisions that can be found in the notes or verified through research. Do not broaden work merely to keep agents busy.

The homepage presents the whole stylist vision once; stages unlock within it. Stage1 is the deployed fallback. Later work serves the full on-user consultation, explicit satisfaction, personal editorial and optional boutique.
