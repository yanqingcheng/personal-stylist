# How we work for this hackathon

## Objective and product authority

We are here to win the hackathon by delivering the distinctive experience the product owner envisions. Her vision, taste and judgement of what is cool and good enough guide product choices. Agents implement, investigate facts, make proposals visible early and explain concrete constraints. Do not substitute a generic safer-looking product, smaller hidden requirement, or agent aesthetic preference for her intent. Winning is the objective, not a promised outcome.

## The loop for each increment

1. Identify the user intent served, using current-decisions.md and the relevant original notes. Separate explicit decisions from our interpretation. If the intent is already documented, use it rather than asking again.
2. Propose the smallest concrete slice that demonstrates it. State material assumptions early so the owner can redirect while independent work continues. No silent narrowing of acceptance: continued exploration until satisfaction or budget pause stays that way.
3. Assign a bounded bead with owner, file scope, dependencies, stopping boundary and observable outcome. Parallelise UI, infrastructure, prompts and experiments where independent; prevent overlapping writers. Commit and push recoverable checkpoints.
4. Show actual screenshots or generated examples promptly, labelled if synthetic. Ask for judgement on concrete work; do not make the owner log in and traverse the app for every visual decision. When she chooses to test the flow herself, make access straightforward.
5. Review the result against intent, not just code correctness. State which requested experience is demonstrated, what is missing or different, and whether that difference was accepted. A build passing, one generated picture, or one revision is not proof of the consultation experience.
6. Apply her feedback, perform proportionate verification, integrate and continue. Capture new decisions in the authoritative entry point and communicate to affected workers; prevent stale plans from silently regaining authority.

## What we can simplify

Hackathon work may use narrow implementations, manual operations, labelled precomputed demo assets and clearly disabled unfinished features. Skip exhaustive edge-case suites, speculative abstractions, generic frameworks, repeated unchanged checks and internal-state tests with no meaningful user consequence. Use the least testing that provides credible evidence for the actual risk. Do not manufacture false results, expose private material or enable unbounded spend to save time.

## What we cannot cut

Review against the owner's intent is mandatory. The owner judges product appeal and good enough; agents distinguish that judgement from facts about implemented behaviour, privacy and money. Bring material tradeoffs to her promptly with a concrete recommendation, not an invented approval ceremony. Never quietly declare an unmet requirement completed. Keep original evidence, uncertainty, user preferences and model interpretation distinct.

## Coordination and communication

Astra parent stays available for high-level reasoning, requirements and product conversation. Sol leads implementation, integration and intent review. Terra handles bounded mechanical tasks. Astra owns creative design, high-level design and visual/product judgement. Use the existing shared beads tracker; no extra process system. Exact ownership can change when explicitly handed over.

No avoidable downtime: when a task is blocked, continue independent work and tell the owner immediately what is needed, what it blocks and what continues. Do not leave workers waiting on decisions that can be found in the notes or verified through research. Do not broaden work merely to keep agents busy.

The homepage presents the whole stylist vision once; stages unlock within it. Stage1 is the deployed fallback. Later work serves the full on-user consultation, explicit satisfaction, personal editorial and optional boutique. Select the demo journey by 14:45 London, begin filming at 15:00, aim for a usable take by 15:30, edit by 16:00, review/refine by 16:20, and export/upload by 16:35 before the 17:00 deadline. Record concrete Codex contributions and honest feature availability.

## Work as a collaborative swarm

For this final hackathon build window, actively use parallel agents where they improve speed and quality. Collaborate directly across lanes: share discoveries early, ask peers for concrete help, challenge assumptions, and spot each other's mistakes. Do not route every exchange through the parent or treat file ownership as a reason to withhold useful criticism. Keep one writer per file/worktree; agree ownership before editing another lane. Sol coordinates integration; the parent remains available for the owner's vision and decisions.

Be creative in finding the fastest convincing demonstration of the intended experience. A worker may propose a better approach or identify a missed requirement outside its assignment; communicate it promptly without silently broadening edits. Cross-check high-impact visual and reasoning outputs with independent native image judgements when useful. Agreement is evidence of consistency, not proof of accuracy. Avoid mandatory review rounds for trivial changes.

The practical concurrency limit is workstation health. Reuse sessions, avoid duplicate dev servers and concurrent heavyweight builds, and check resource pressure before increasing concurrency. Reduce heavy jobs if the machine becomes strained; never trade workstation stability for nominal agent count. Paid API gates and the total budget remain unchanged.

Use a lightweight 2×2 for significant failures: how this specific mistake was introduced, how it escaped detection, the general pattern that introduced it, and the general check that would catch it. Apply the smallest useful prevention to the next increment; do not turn learning into a separate bureaucracy.

## Learn from each real journey

Each bounded brief states Qing's intended outcome, a concrete demonstration and the known gap. Review the delivered interaction against that outcome before calling it ready. After live feedback, capture the cause and smallest prevention, then apply it in the next iteration; keep the process light.

For this project: show the photo limit and live upload progress in the phone viewport and exercise a normal 2+2 upload; trace a visible drape selection through to its saved canonical colour in both placements; assess the colour journey against multiple-photo hypotheses, an accepted neutral-light composite, targeted comparisons and a supported result rather than a fixed quiz and template list; and inspect screenshots for the next action being visible. Distinguish what is implemented from what has actually been demonstrated.

For material misses, capture four short lessons: how this instance was introduced, how it escaped detection, the general failure mode, and a reusable detection check. For the drapes: reversed panels retained fixed-order controls; label/code checks missed the spatial choice; display and action order drifted apart; select each visible option and verify the saved meaning in both orders. Apply the same visible-choice-to-saved-meaning check to outfit-sheet selection and revision.
